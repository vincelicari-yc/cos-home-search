# Deal-Breakers & Red Flags

Almost entirely from `[T04]` ("NEVER Buy These Types of Homes"), the single most useful
transcript for our search, cross-confirmed by `[T05]`–`[T08]`. Anything tagged `[BASE]` only is
still unconfirmed baseline — treat it as a lead, not a fact.

**The through-line across every transcript:** the expensive mistakes in this market aren't
bad houses, they're **carrying costs nobody quoted you before you were under contract** —
insurance, foundation, roof, drainage. Every one of these is knowable in advance.

---

## 1. Expansive soil & foundation — the biggest single risk

Colorado Springs sits on **Pierre shale and bentonite clay** running the length of the Front
Range. That clay **swells up to 20% in volume** when wet and contracts when dry, working on
foundations season after season. `[T04]` `[BASE]`

**Roughly 25% of Colorado Springs homes experience some foundation issue in their lifetime.**
`[T04]`

Highest risk where steep terrain meets clay near the mountains: **Rockrimmon, Broadmoor Bluffs,
and the southwest foothills** `[T04]`. Rockrimmon and Broadmoor Bluffs are outside our radius,
but *our whole search area is west-central, near the foothills* — so this applies to us directly.

### Look for
- Stair-step cracking in foundation walls or brick, especially at corners `[T04]` `[BASE]`
- Drywall cracks radiating diagonally from door and window corners `[BASE]`
- Doors and windows that stick or won't latch `[BASE]`
- Sloping floors — a marble test tells you a lot in 30 seconds `[BASE]`
- Basement slab heave, especially under interior bearing walls `[BASE]`

### What the transcripts add that changes our strategy
> **A documented prior repair is a POSITIVE, not a disqualifier.** `[T04]`
> "The best purchases in soil risk areas are the ones where the foundation has already been
> repaired, documented, and warrantied." You know the problem was addressed. Also good: a newer
> build with an **engineered foundation** designed for Front Range clay.

> **Foundation problems kill FHA and VA loans.** `[T04]` They're one of the most common reasons
> those loans get complicated or denied — the loan may not be approved until the issue is
> resolved, and because that rarely happens before closing, **the deal falls apart.** If we're
> using conventional financing this is less acute, but it still shrinks our future buyer pool.

### Cost
$3,700–$4,900 typical (cited from 172 completed projects); broader range $2,000–$16,000; she's
seen $25,000 on a single property, and her own summary puts the tail at **well over $100,000**
`[T04]`. Wide spread — the number depends entirely on scope, so price it per house.

### Ask
Has a structural engineer ever evaluated this home? Is there a soils report? Any repair history,
is it permitted and documented, and **is the warranty transferable?**

## 2. Wildfire, insurance, and insurability

**This is the item most likely to break our $600k budget, and it's live inside our radius.**

Named wildland–urban interface neighborhoods: **Mountain Shadows**, Black Forest, Cedar Heights,
**Peregrine**, parts of Rockrimmon and Flying Horse `[T04]`. Mountain Shadows is **6.6 minutes
from our anchor** — well inside our radius. See `30-our-radius-neighborhoods.md`.

- Mountain Shadows lost **346 homes** in Waldo Canyon (2012); Black Forest lost **509** (2013) `[T04]`
- High-risk fire zone premiums commonly **$6,500–$15,000+/yr**, some homes $7,000–$22,000 `[T04]`
- Mountain Shadows specifically: risen from **$1,800–$2,800 pre-crisis to $4,500–$8,500/yr** —
  about **$300–$600/month added to the payment** `[T06]`
- Statewide, Colorado premiums are **up 58% since 2018**, averaging $4,200–$5,200/yr `[T05]`
- **Some carriers stopped writing new policies statewide; others paused new business in high-risk
  wildfire areas back in 2023** `[T04]`
- Fallback is the **Colorado FAIR Plan**, insurer of last resort — expensive, more limited `[T04]`
- **Lenders and insurers are both increasingly cautious** in these zones — a combination that's
  hard for a neighborhood to overcome `[T05]`

### The lever we can actually pull
**Colorado HB25-1182 took effect January this year:** insurers must be transparent about their
wildfire risk models and must provide a clear pathway to premium reductions through
property-specific mitigation `[T04]`. So defensible space, non-combustible roofing, and
ember-resistant vents are **financial instruments, not just safety features** `[T04]`.

`[T07]` puts a number on it: **one house at a $7,000 premium next door to one at $2,500,
because they cleared the scrub oak properly.** Documented mitigation is worth thousands a year.

### The rule
> **Get an insurance quote for the specific address before the inspection deadline — ideally
> before making the offer.** `[T04]` `[T07]`
> An uninsurable house is an unbuyable house, and "the Zillow monthly payment estimate is almost
> never accurate" `[T06]`. More than a third of current Mountain Shadows residents weren't there
> for the fire and don't know the backstory until a lender asks for a binder `[T06]`.

Expect the insurer or lender to require **cutting back large trees** — which also helps the
foundation `[T04]`.

## 3. Flood zones and water

Fountain Creek runs from Manitou Springs through the heart of the city along Hwy 24 and feeds
**Monument Creek** `[T04]`. **Monument Creek runs right through our search area, along the I-25
corridor near Pope's Bluff** — so this is not a hypothetical for us.

The Waldo Canyon and Black Forest burn scars stripped hillside vegetation, worsening runoff
`[T04]`. In Mountain Shadows, **23% of properties face severe flood risk** precisely because the
fire removed what used to absorb rain and snowmelt `[T06]`.

- Flood insurance adds **$1,000–$5,000+/yr** `[T04]`
- Flood-zone homes can **appraise lower** than comparable homes outside the zone → financing
  complications now, and a smaller buyer pool when we sell `[T04]`
- Your insurer may require added flood coverage simply for being **next to a creek or small body
  of water** `[T03]` `[T06]`

### The distinction that matters
> A home can sit inside the mapped flood zone **horizontally** and still be **elevated above the
> actual flood risk** `[T04]`. Check elevation relative to the waterway, and prefer properties
> that already have **elevation documentation** in hand — it changes the picture completely.

**Pull the FEMA map for every address.** `[T06]` cites a Rockrimmon buyer who skipped that
conversation and at underwriting was **quoted more than double** the budgeted insurance.

## 4. Drainage, freeze–thaw, and mold

Upstream of both the foundation and the flood item — nearly every foundation problem here starts
as a water problem.

At 6,000+ ft with extreme daily temperature swings, **water that gets in during warm months
freezes in winter, expands, and creates hidden moisture pockets** that fuel mold long after the
original event `[T04]`. Spring snowmelt plus monsoon season drives water at foundations citywide.

> **Be very cautious buying a house at the bottom of a hill.** `[T04]`

### Walk the perimeter yourself, before the inspection `[T04]`
- Ground must slope **away** on all four sides `[T04]` `[T08]` `[BASE]`
- **A downspout terminating 12 inches from the foundation sends every storm straight at the
  wall** `[T04]`
- Window wells: drains present, no staining showing they've held water `[BASE]`
- Crawlspace: vapor barrier, ideally **fully encapsulated with sealed walls** `[T04]`
- **Test the sump pump** — 30 seconds tells you whether the primary water management works `[T04]`
- Look for an **interior basement drainage system** (tile and channel). In a city that gets
  precipitation in bursts with freeze–thaw, a home without one is a risk that may not surface
  until the first heavy rain `[T04]`

### Efflorescence — the tell
White, crystalline, salt-like deposits on basement walls, left behind when water moved through
the concrete or block and evaporated. **The basement can look bone dry today and that
crystallization still proves water has been there.** `[T04]`

### Cost and escalation
Mold remediation $1,200–$3,700 typical; **wall mold behind finished surfaces $1,000–$20,000**
depending on scope `[T04]`. **Remediate without fixing the moisture source and it comes back.**
A manageable **$900 drying job becomes $6,000+ if water sits untreated for just 48 hours** `[T04]`.

Mold testing has to happen inside the inspection deadline, which is legally binding `[T04]`.

## 5. Roof and hail

Colorado Springs averages **5.5 hail days per year**, and at 6,000+ ft UV degrades shingles
significantly faster than at sea level `[T04]`. **Hail is 60–70% of all Colorado insurance
claims** `[T04]`.

### The coverage trap
> Carriers are **moving from replacement cost value to actual cash value** on aging roofs `[T04]`.
> Buy a home with an old or cosmetically patched roof and **you may not be covered for full
> replacement** when the next storm hits — you get depreciated value instead.

### Cost
**$11,000–$25,000** for most homes in 2026; her own recent replacement $13,000; another quote
$15,000 for a ~2,800 sqft home `[T04]`. (She also cites El Paso County permit data at
$28,000–$38,000, which contradicts her own figures — treat that number as unreliable.)

### Class 4 is the play
A **Class 4 impact-resistant roof saves 20–30% on the insurance premium** and lasts longer, in a
place that gets hail roughly every 5 years `[T04]`. `[T08]` confirms shingle type **drastically**
changes the policy amount.

> **Negotiation tactic `[T04]`:** don't ask the seller for "a roof replacement" — ask for a
> **Class 4 roof specifically**. It saves us money every year we own the house, not just once.

Want: permitted work, manufacturer's warranty, Class 4 materials.

### Look for
Soft-metal bruising on gutters, downspouts, vents, flashing, and AC condenser fins — it reveals
hail history even when the shingles look passable `[T04]` `[BASE]`. Multiple shingle layers at
the edges signal cheap prior work `[BASE]`. For stucco, check for **hail pock marks and
cracking**; badly faded paint means at minimum deferred maintenance `[T08]`.

## 6. Aging systems in 1970s–80s stock

Our budget lands squarely in this era. `[T06]` warns that buyers from newer-build markets are
caught off guard by what older stock costs: **a kitchen remodel averages ~$25,000 in Colorado,
a full professional remodel up to $60/sqft**, and it runs *higher* in older neighborhoods because
of older building systems and hidden structural surprises.

> **"A home listed at [$450k] that needs $80,000 of work is not a [$450k] home."** `[T06]`

| Issue | Era | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Aluminum branch wiring | ~1965–1975 | Fire risk at connections; insurers may refuse or require remediation `[BASE]` |
| Polybutylene supply pipe | ~1978–1995 | Gray/blue plastic, fails without warning, often uninsurable `[BASE]` |
| Galvanized supply pipe | pre-1960s | Corrodes shut, kills pressure `[BASE]` |
| Cast-iron / clay sewer lateral | pre-1980 | Root intrusion and collapse. **Always scope the sewer** `[BASE]` |
| Original furnace / water heater | any | 15–20 yr life; altitude affects combustion tuning `[BASE]` |
| Federal Pacific / Zinsco panel | 1950s–80s | Breakers that fail to trip; full replacement `[BASE]` |

Also expect sellers in older established areas to be **retired, on fixed budgets, long-tenured,
and unwilling to renovate** `[T03]` — so assume we inherit the deferred maintenance and price it
into the offer.

## 7. Utility district and the monthly bill

Genuinely surprising, and cheap to check.

> Inside the Colorado Springs Utilities footprint **for all services**, rates are among the most
> competitive in Colorado. **Cross into a private water co-op, or have CSU provide some services
> but not all, and monthly carrying costs can jump by hundreds of dollars.** `[T07]`

**Ask: who sends the water bill, and is everything on one bill or several?** `[T07]`
Also confirm city sewer vs. septic — more likely at our radius edges `[BASE]`.

And get **12 months of actual bills**, a cleaner read on real efficiency than any listing claim
`[BASE]`.

## 8. Metro districts and HOAs — the hidden monthly cost

Not a big risk in older west-central stock (most predates metro districts), but verify per
address, because **the cost is invisible on the listing.**

- A metro district is **not** an HOA, doesn't show up as dues, and adds to the mill levy `[BASE]`
- Banning Lewis Ranch: HOA $84–$129/mo **plus** ~$100/mo metro district = **$184–$229/month of
  governance cost before base property tax** `[T06]`
- `[T02]` describes a house with higher-than-normal taxes, **two HOAs, and a metro district**
- **Property tax rates vary widely between zip codes inside the same city limits**, driven by
  school district, metro district, and special-district overlays tied to the specific address `[T06]`
- Colorado Springs is full of "little villages" — HOA status, rules, and taxes **vary street to
  street inside a single named neighborhood** `[T03]`

### If we ever look at a condo or townhome `[T04]`
Unlikely at 4bd/3ba, but for completeness: fees average $200–$600/mo. HOA insurance deductibles
have shifted from flat-rate to **percentage-based** because of hail and wildfire, so one major
claim can produce a **six-figure deductible passed to owners as a special assessment**. Colorado
has **no law universally requiring reserve studies** on a fixed schedule. Ask for financial
statements, the reserve study, the **last 12 months of board meeting minutes** (they show what
the board has been avoiding), and any pending special assessment — which must be disclosed
honestly under CCIOA. Walk the common areas *before* the unit; neglected landscaping, lights
out, and chipped paint mean walk away. Condos and townhomes were **hit hardest of any segment
in 2025** `[T05]`.

`[T04]`'s own tally, which is roughly our repair-reserve rationale in one place:

| Risk | Annual or one-time cost |
| --- | --- |
| Flood insurance | ~$5,000/yr |
| Foundation repair | $3,000 to well over $100,000 |
| Wildfire insurance premium | $15,000+/yr in high-risk zones |
| Roof replacement | up to ~$30,000 |
| HOA special assessment | arrives with no warning |
| Mold remediation | escalates fast if the source isn't found |
