## The headline: we are buying into a buyer's market

- COS median prices **fell 5% citywide in 2025** (Colorado Association of Realtors) `[T05]`
- **Q1 2026: average new-listing price $618,000 vs. average sold price $540,000 — a $79,000 gap.**
  "Sellers are listing at 2022 prices and buyers are buying at 2026 prices." `[T05]`
- **Contract fallout rate at an all-time high** — homes go under contract and then fall out,
  because the seller won't fix anything and the buyer feels they're overpaying `[T05]`
- By late 2025 the median El Paso County home took about **2 months to sell**, up year over year;
  **buyers have more leverage than any time since before the pandemic** `[T02]` `[T05]`
- Showings per day dropped **35–40%** from early fall 2025 to mid-winter `[T05]`
- Citywide appreciation forecast **2–4% through 2026** `[T05]`
- Only **3,200 permits** in the past 12 months, down from prior years `[T05]`

### That $79k list-to-sold gap is the single most actionable number in the batch
It says asking prices in this market are aspirational and **offering under list is normal, not
insulting.** It also means a home listed at $649k may well be a $570k house — so we should not
treat our $600k ceiling as a filter on *list price* alone. **Filter on list, but evaluate on what
it should actually sell for.**

## Timing: we're entering at a good moment, and it may not last

- We're in **August**, past the **April–June peak military PCS window** when demand and seller
  confidence are highest `[T05]` `[BASE]`. Fall and winter mean less competition.
- But the leverage window is fragile. In **February this year rates briefly dipped below 6% and
  pending sales jumped 18%, closed sales over 30%** — from one rate move `[T05]`. There's
  substantial pent-up demand sitting on the sidelines watching rates.
- **December 2025: 909 single-family sales, +8.6% YoY, a four-year December high** `[T05]` —
  this is a market stabilizing and building a base, not falling.
- `[T05]`'s read: the forces making 2026 feel sluggish are **setting up a tighter market by late
  2026 into 2027.**

> **Implication:** negotiate hard, but don't wait indefinitely for a better market. The right
> house at a payment that works is better than a perfect market that closes before we act.

## How to structure the offer

### The rate buydown beats the price cut
> **"A $15,000 rate buydown will often save you more money every month than a $15,000 price
> reduction."** `[T05]`

Worth modeling both ways on any house we get serious about. A price cut lowers the loan slightly;
a buydown lowers the rate for the life of the loan (or for the buydown period). At current rates
the monthly difference is usually meaningful.

### Ask for the right things, in this order
1. **A Class 4 impact-resistant roof**, not just "a roof replacement" `[T04]`. Saves 20–30% on
   insurance every year we own it, not once. This is the highest-leverage single ask.
2. **Rate buydown** over price reduction `[T05]`.
3. **Seller concessions toward closing costs** — commonly **$8,000–$10,000** in this market `[T05]`.
4. **Repair credits** for anything the inspection turns up `[T05]`.

### Keep a real repair reserve
`[T08]`'s core warning: **don't buy anything that only makes sense if appreciation saves you.**
His prediction is that COS **may stagnate or stay flat for the next year or so**.

So: buy at a payment that works **today**, on today's rate, with today's insurance quote. Don't
assume appreciation, and **don't assume rates come down so we can refinance** `[T08]`.

Working target given the $600k ceiling and 1970s–80s stock: **offer in the $540–570k range and
reserve $25–40k** for roof, sewer, radon, and drainage. The $79k list-to-sold gap `[T05]` says
that's a realistic ask, not a lowball.

### Financing note
**El Paso County conforming loan limit is a little over $800k** `[T05]` — comfortably above our
budget, so no jumbo-loan complexity.

If we use **FHA or VA**: foundation issues are one of the most common reasons those loans get
complicated or killed `[T04]`. That raises the stakes on the structural inspection considerably,
and makes a **documented, warrantied prior repair** more valuable than an unknown-condition
foundation.

## Exit strategy — ask before buying, not after

`[T08]`'s three questions, worth answering honestly for every serious candidate:

1. **If we had to move quickly, could we sell this and recover our money?**
2. **If not, is the loss small enough to accept?**
3. **Could we rent it out instead?**

This is where the `[T08]` supply thesis pays off for us: built-out west-central has no
new-construction pipeline undercutting resale, so our exit is structurally better than the
northern fringe's. See `30-our-radius-neighborhoods.md`.

## Insurance is now a market force, not a line item

Worth its own heading because it's changed the math citywide:

- Colorado homeowner premiums **up 58% since 2018**, averaging **$4,200–$5,200/yr statewide** `[T05]`
- Wildfire-risk areas run to **$15,000/yr** `[T05]`; Mountain Shadows specifically $4,500–$8,500 `[T06]`
- "**I'm seeing this kill deals left and right.**" `[T05]`
- **Lenders and insurers are both** getting cautious in exposed zones — a combination a
  neighborhood can't easily overcome `[T05]`
- Condos and townhomes were **hit hardest of any segment in 2025**, from HOA fee increases
  stacked on the insurance rise `[T05]`

**Get the address-specific quote before the inspection deadline.** See `20-deal-breakers.md`.

## Longer-term context

Reasons to think demand holds up over our ownership horizon:

- El Paso County projected past **1 million residents by 2050**, +10–15k/yr `[T05]`
- Citywide housing shortage of roughly **27,000 units** `[T01]` `[T05]`
- **Elevate Downtown**, a 10–15 year revitalization plan launched February this year — the first
  serious planning-level attention to urban-proximate housing in decades `[T05]`
- Multifamily pipeline winding down: ~**1,300 units this year, 1,700 next** `[T05]` — less
  competing supply
- Five military installations, 100,000+ defense-related jobs, a **$7 billion aerospace economy**
  providing a stable demand floor `[T05]`
- **Peak Innovation Park / Swire Coca-Cola**: $475M, 620,000 sqft, broke ground March this year;
  170 permanent jobs plus ~1,200 construction jobs; Amazon, Northrop Grumman, and Aerospace Corp
  already on site `[T01]`
- Airport modernization: $75M federal investment, first direct international flight 2025 `[T01]`

Most of this lands on the north and east sides, not ours. It matters to us indirectly — as
citywide demand support and as a reason the *northern* fringe keeps absorbing new supply while
our area doesn't.

### Costs to keep an eye on
**Colorado Springs Utilities is in a $1.1 billion capital buildout** `[T01]` — fiber $411M total
($295M 2025–2029), wastewater expansion, electric capacity for up to 600 MW. CSU is city-owned
and bills all four services together `[BASE]`, and buildouts of this size eventually show up in
rates. Not a reason to avoid anything, but a reason to look at **12 months of actual bills**
rather than assuming today's rate holds.

Colorado roads got a **D+** in the 2025 ASCE report card, with only 34% in good condition `[T05]`.

> expand Montgomery Reservoir. These may be different scopes described loosely rather than a true
> contradiction, but neither number is verified. Low stakes for our decision — recorded for
> honesty rather than because it changes anything.
