# Our Radius vs. What the Videos Cover

**The most important finding from the transcript batch: six of the eight videos are about
neighborhoods we cannot buy in.**

`[T01]`–`[T06]` are by a north-side specialist. Her focus — Flying Horse, Cordera, Wolf Ranch,
Northgate, Pine Creek, Briargate, Monument, Black Forest, Banning Lewis Ranch, Fountain — sits
**20–30 minutes from our anchor at $800k–$1.5M**. Both the distance and the price put essentially
all of it out of reach. Her *risk* material (`20-deal-breakers.md`) and *market data*
(`40-market-and-negotiation.md`) transfer completely. Her neighborhood recommendations don't.

`[T07]` and `[T08]`, from the other channel, are the most useful for us, because they're about
how to evaluate any house — and their central thesis favors our area. See below.

---

## The good news: our area is on the right side of the structural shift

`[T08]` makes the strongest claim in the whole batch:

> **"If you buy in an area where they can easily build more homes, it will definitely affect your
> appreciation. Period."** New construction controls pricing for its area — as long as new homes
> are an option for buyers, **a resale can't appreciate past new-build pricing.**

His prescription is to buy **where they can't build anymore**, and he names exactly our kind of
area: **"west-side neighborhoods, maybe some downtown neighborhoods,"** places already built out
or where subdividing would be a very large lift. The east side toward Falcon is easily dividable
land, so **less appreciation** `[T08]`.

`[T05]` describes the same dynamic from the other direction: the 40-year northward-expansion
model is breaking down under land cost, infrastructure limits, insurance, permitting friction,
and affordability — and **"the communities that already have their infrastructure, their
amenities, and their location advantages built in become more valuable, not less. You can't
replicate what took 20 or 30 years to build."**

`[T06]` gives the concrete counter-example: in Banning Lewis Ranch, builders are still selling
new construction from the mid-$300s with heavy incentives, so **a resale bought today competes
directly against brand-new builds in three years, suppressing appreciation.**

**Our entire search radius is built-out, west-central, 1970s–80s Colorado Springs. There is no
meaningful new-construction pipeline competing with us here.** That's a real structural advantage
we should stop treating as a limitation.

The tradeoff, stated honestly: our area is **District 11**, and `[T07]` notes that D-20/D-12/D-38
homes hold price better when the market cools. See the schools note at the bottom.

## Mountain Shadows: inside our radius, and the one neighborhood the videos warn hardest about

It's 6.6 minutes from YWAM with mountain views, which makes it genuinely tempting. Here's the
full picture `[T06]` `[T04]` `[T07]`:

- **100% of properties are at wildfire risk over the next 30 years** (Redfin data) `[T06]`
- **23% face severe flood risk on top of that** — because Waldo Canyon removed the vegetation
  that used to absorb rain and snowmelt `[T06]`
- Lost **346 homes** in the 2012 Waldo Canyon fire; much of it is rebuilt stock `[T04]` `[T06]`
- Insurance risen from **$1,800–$2,800 pre-crisis to $4,500–$8,500/yr** — roughly
  **$300–$600/month added to the payment** `[T06]`
- **Median sale price over the last 12 months: about $620,000** `[T06]` — **above our $600k
  ceiling before we even add the insurance**
- District 11; Chipeta Elementary rates 9/10 `[T06]`
- Her verdict: the most visually compelling neighborhood for out-of-state buyers browsing online,
  and **"the widest gap between what buyers expect and what they're actually buying into"** `[T06]`

### Our position
**Don't rule it out, but invert the order of operations.** Normally we'd tour, then get an
insurance quote. Here, **get the quote for the specific address first** — before touring, before
falling for a view. If the premium lands at $4,500–$8,500/yr, that's $375–$700/month on top of
principal, interest, and taxes, and it has to come out of what we can borrow.

Two things could still make a Mountain Shadows home work:
1. **Documented recent fire mitigation.** `[T07]`'s example is a $7,000 premium next door to a
   $2,500 premium purely because one owner cleared the scrub oak properly. Under HB25-1182
   insurers must now offer a mitigation pathway to lower premiums `[T04]`.
2. **A price well under median.** At $620k median we're shopping the bottom of that market, which
   in practice means the homes needing the most work — exactly where the deferred-maintenance
   math gets dangerous.

## Vista Grande / 80918 — the most interesting lead in the batch

`[T05]` names **80918 (Vista Grande)** first on its list of zip codes where buyers currently have
**the most price cuts, the most days on market, and the most negotiating room.** Vista Grande
measures **3.3 minutes from our anchor.**

The same passage is candid about why: these are neighborhoods that "rode the old northward
expansion wave" and are now the **"softening middle"** — not luxury enough to have irreplaceable
infrastructure, not cheap enough to absorb buyer hesitation. So the leverage is real, and so is
the weaker near-term appreciation.

**For us that tradeoff reads favorably.** We're buying a home to live in near YWAM, not a
short-hold appreciation play, and `[T08]` explicitly warns against buying anything that only
works if appreciation saves you. Maximum negotiating leverage 3 minutes from the anchor is worth
real attention.

> **Verify per address.** The 80918 zip is large and extends east of I-25 beyond our radius. Our
> Vista Grande reference point measures 3.3 minutes, but "in 80918" does not by itself mean
> "within 10 minutes." Run `./tools/intake.py` on every specific address.

## Also inside the radius, worth flagging

**Old Colorado City / the west side.** `[T07]` describes the character honestly: older homes,
floor plans that "can be really funky," but it **feels like a mountain town** — 20-year-old
coffee shops, quick access to some of the best Front Range trails. Its warning is about fit, not
quality: "don't buy this modern house if you love the historic feel," and vice versa. Two
cautions for us: it measures **10.0 minutes**, which breaks the rule in real traffic, and the
**Fountain Creek / Hwy 24 corridor is the named flood corridor** `[T04]`.

**Pinecliff and Woodmen Valley.** Neither is discussed directly, but both sit foothills-adjacent
at 8.4 and 9.2 minutes. Treat both as **wildfire-insurance-verify** before touring, and check
well vs. city water at the Woodmen Valley edge.

**Kissing Camels.** 9.0 minutes, but almost certainly above $600k for 4bd/3ba.

## Schools — reference only, but with one honest caveat

We don't score schools, since you're homeschooling. But `[T07]` makes a point worth recording:
**even without kids, districts move resale value** — D-20, D-12, and D-38 homes hold price better
when the market cools.

**Our entire search radius is District 11.** `[T06]` cites D-11 districtwide proficiency at
**28.1% math and 36.7% ELA**. That's a mild resale headwind on everything we'll look at, and it's
partly offset by `[T08]`'s supply argument: built-out west-central can't be undercut by new
construction the way D-20's northern fringe can.

Two related traps from the transcripts, both about verifying per address rather than per
neighborhood:
- **District boundaries run through individual streets.** Part of the Broadmoor area isn't in
  D-12 at all — it's D-2 and parts of D-11 `[T06]`. Verify the exact address, and call the school.
- **Individual school ratings vary sharply within a district.** Mountain Shadows is D-11, yet
  Chipeta Elementary rates 9/10 `[T06]`. Don't judge a specific school by its district average.
